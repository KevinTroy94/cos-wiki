A walled town in the valley, ruled by [[Baron Vargas Vallakovich]]. The road ends at a pair of iron gates, tall and plain and very well kept. Along either side of the approach, rows of pikes are planted in the earth, each topped with the head of a wolf, jaws frozen open, eyes long since gone. Guards in smiling armour man the gates.

![[wmremove-transformed.jpeg|200]]

The smiling-faced sun is everywhere: on the guards' armour, and on the masks some townsfolk wear. In the town square stands a large stage, too high and shallow to be anything other than a repurposed gallows. From there the Baron hears petitions the way a king does, dismissing every complaint with blind optimism, missing pigs, a bakery without flour, stolen church property, and closing each one with "All will be well!" The crowd answers in kind, as though at a church gathering.

[[Morgantha]] said [[Arabelle]] was brought here, taken by [[Izek Strazni]], and that the town militia had her last.

Places the party has visited in town:
- [[Blue Water Inn]], run by the Martikovs
- [[Blinsky's Toys]], the toymaker's shop
- [[The Merry Crust]], [[Bozena]]'s bakery
- [[St. Andral's Church]], tended by [[Father Lucian]]

A sign in town details the methods of "discipline" used by the [[Order of Contentment]]: "Contentment", "Amusement", "Convalescence" and "Dischargement". Some townsfolk wear strapped masks, called masks of contentment.

The town is said to be warded against vampires. According to Father Lucian, those wards depend on the bones of St. Andral, which have been stolen from the church's crypt.

Outside the walls, an hour's walk north, stands [[Sunset Lodge]], one of the town's punishments. Kozlov Manor, in town, has stood empty since its family was sent out to the lodge three years ago.

> Vallaki was founded not long after Strahd's armies conquered the valley by an ancestor of the town's current burgomaster, Baron Vargas Vallakovich. The Vallakoviches have royal blood in their veins and have long believed themselves superior to the Zarovich line. Baron Vallakovich has deluded himself into believing that hope and happiness are the keys to Vallaki's salvation. He stages one festival after another to bolster the spirits of the townsfolk, but most Vallakians consider these festivals to be pointless, meaningless affairs more likely to incur Strahd's wrath than to provide any hope for the future.
>
> In the last festival, Baron Vallakovich had townsfolk parade through the streets with the severed heads of wolves on pikes. His next event, which the burgomaster has dubbed the Festival of the Blazing Sun, is soon to get under way.
