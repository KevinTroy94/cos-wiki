[[Father Lucian]] began to lead the group down to the undercroft of [[St. Andral's Church|St. Andral’s]]. He turned a crank at the side of a modest pipe organ set into the wall behind the altar. Once this was done, he played a note on the keys, and the altar clicked, allowing it to be slid on rails along a metal track to reveal a stair down into the hidden undercroft. At the bottom of the stairs they found a glass cloche dome over bare satin. The glass case where the bones should have lain stood open and empty.

![[image-removebg-preview(13).png]]

Father Lucian explained that he hadn’t checked on the bones in three days. They were the bones of Saint Andral, who died to keep the land that became [[Vallaki]] safe from an overwhelming force.

Virro found it suspicious that anyone would know how to enter the crypt, and he asked Lucian whether anyone else knew the bones were down here. Lucian told him that he had thought he was the only one who knew how to enter the undercroft. He noted that a decoy grave for Saint Andral, there to deter grave robbers, had been dug up the night before, and this was what had prompted him to check the undercroft.

Lucian gave the party the book recording the interments in the cemetery, including a crossed-out entry and reused plots.

Virro began to inspect the walls and found what the thieves had used. Behind a section of stone lay a tunnel. Edric and Ignacious followed it through the dark, out beyond the town’s bounds, until it opened near a small whitewashed cabin with a sun painted over the door: [[Sunset Lodge]]. [[Ireena Kolyana|Ireena]] stayed back in the church.

Elsewhere in Vallaki, the [[Blue Water Inn]] had descended into chaos. Retnuh moved through the brawl trying to check on [[Karl Wachter]], but kept being assaulted by patrons and by [[Nikolai Wachter]], who tried to kill him with a barstool.

Soon the whole room was fighting itself, patron against patron, bottles flying and tables splintering. During the fight, two hunters approached the bar, and Retnuh pleaded with them to help Karl. The larger of them, [[Szoldar Szoldarovich|Szoldar]], headed off to find a doctor, while the smaller, [[Yevgeni Krushkin|Yevgeni]], tried to subdue the rowdier patrons. Several of the inn’s quieter drinkers stepped in without a word and pinned [[Volenta Popofsky|Volenta]]’s arms. [[Urwin Martikov|Urwin]], calm as ever, settled the whole room with a crossbow aimed at the crowd.

![[Pasted image 20261020202353.png]]

The moment she was released, Volenta vanished through the door.

Retnuh went looking for answers and found [[Bozena]] at her bakery. She told him of Sunset Lodge and the kinds of punishment meted out in Vallaki, and of Kozlov Manor, the empty manor belonging to the first family to be sent out to the lodge, three years ago. Bozena mentioned that someone had agreed to buy it, and assumed that the party was part of that group. Retnuh set off north and walked until he found the rest of the party at the lodge, north of the town’s gates.

The lodge was a small whitewashed cabin about an hour’s walk outside Vallaki, standing alone in a clearing of birch trees where the forest goes unnaturally quiet.

At the lodge, the party began to look around. On the porch, a row of boots and slippers waited beside the entrance, neatly ordered heel to heel, visibly cleaned and cared for. Virro noted a fine pale grit on the porch and looked up to see four statues of crouched creatures with smiling, sun-faced heads on the lodge roof, their grinning faces weathered pale.

Inside, the lodge was a single neatly kept room, spotless and tightly made, with a guest book on the table and a shelf of mementos from former guests. Among them sat a pocket watch, its case engraved with the name **P. Hask**. Ignacious frowned at it. He had seen that name before, in the register of burials at St. Andral’s.

While the others searched the cabin, Edric noticed the [[Animated Rug of Smothering|rug]] at its centre. It was a handsome thing, rich with wine-dark reds and a border of faded gold, and so clean that it looked out of place in a room that was otherwise coated in dust. He bent and took it by a corner tassel. The rug woke in his hand and reared up like a cobra, its whole length lifting off the floor in a single serpentine motion, the fringe whipping and the pattern rippling along its back as though it were scales. The rug dropped over him and coiled around him, drawing tight.

![[Pasted image 20261020205241.png]]

Outside, something struck the porch with a heavy, deliberate thud. Then another. Then a third. The four stone sun-faced figures that had crouched on the roof had unfurled themselves into monstrous [[Sunset Gargoyle|Sunset Gargoyles]].

The first one crashed through a window in a burst of glass and splintered frame, and its claws closed around Ignacious’ chest. It hauled him up and out through the broken pane. The cabin dropped away beneath him. In the roar of the air, a thought came to him. [[Pieter Hask]], the name on the pocket watch and in the burial register, had a cause of death written beside it: _a fall._ He had not fallen. He had been taken up, just as Ignacious was being taken now.

High above the treeline, a second gargoyle had launched after him. He drew his blade, hacked through his captor’s grip and plummeted toward the second gargoyle, crashing through the roof. Ignacious didn’t feel the floor when he landed. He was already unconscious.

At the front door, a gargoyle had planted itself in the doorway and blocked the way out. Retnuh charged it.
